# FERRITE v0.10.0

## Game modes

Four ways to play, chosen by the host, by a rotation, or by the lobby's vote before each round.

- **Conquest** (long). The original: five points taken in order along one front line, decided on tickets. Unchanged.
- **Team Deathmatch** (fast). No objectives. Both sides deploy forward, round B and D, and are back three seconds after falling. First side to the kill target wins: eight kills per player a side (96 for 12 v 12), or the lead when the eight-minute clock runs out.
- **Capture the Flag** (slow). Each side's flag stands on B or D. Walk onto theirs to take it and carry it to your own stand to score; three captures win, or the lead after twelve minutes. A carrier who falls drops the flag where they lay: its own side returns it by touching it, and it goes home by itself after 25 seconds. Each side redeploys together every twelve seconds, so a won firefight opens a gap to run through.
- **Stronghold** (medium). One point, C, open to both sides at all times. Every second you hold it scores a point; 180 wins, or the lead after eight minutes.

HOST & PLAY has a GAME MODE row: the four modes, or MIXED for a different one each round. The lobby shows the modes under the maps: click one to vote. A dedicated server takes `--mode conquest|tdm|ctf|stronghold`, `--mode-rotation all|off|a,b,c`, `--score N` and `--time SECS` (the target and the clock outside conquest), and `--modes` lists them. The server list shows what each game is playing.

Bots play all four: they hunt in a deathmatch, mass on the centre in Stronghold, and in Capture the Flag run the flag home, chase the enemy carrier and return their own.

## Kill streaks and pickups

- Kills without dying are a streak. At **3** the one who fell leaves **armour** where they lay, at **5** **overdrive**, at **7** **overcharge**, and at 10 and every five after it overcharge and armour together. The streak is announced to everyone, and whoever ends a streak of five or more is paid 100 points for the shutdown.
- Any other kill has about a one in five chance of leaving a **health pack** or a **resupply**.
- Walk over a pickup to take it. Anyone can, friend or enemy, so a reward has to be fetched. They lie for 30 to 40 seconds, show on the minimap, and are marked on screen once you are close.

| Pickup | What it does |
|---|---|
| Health pack | +60 health, up to your class's maximum |
| Armour | +50 armour (100 at most). Armour takes six parts in ten of every hit until it is gone |
| Resupply | Both weapons reloaded, frags refilled |
| Overdrive | Twelve seconds of the capture surge: faster on your feet, half-time reloads |
| Overcharge | Ten seconds of half again the damage |

Armour and overcharge are lost when you fall. `--no-drops` on a dedicated server turns pickups off.

## Credits and the armory

- Every round you finish pays credits: **120 to 240 for a win, 45 to 165 for a loss**. The base is 120 or 45; your score in the round adds up to 120 more. Joining late pays in proportion to how much of the round you played, and so does a round shorter than three minutes.
- The result screen shows what the round paid, and the lobby shows your balance.
- **ARMORY**, on the home screen, sells seven new weapon finishes (Emberfall, Cobalt Steel, Carbon, Toxic, Rose Gold, Glacier, Gilded) and six new soldier looks (Blackout, Pathfinder, Juggernaut, Wraith, Warlord, Meltdown) for 350 to 1500 credits. A soldier stands on the map wearing what you are looking at, and the gun turns beside the list. A finish is bought once and can go on any gun, or on every gun at a click.
- Everything that existed before v0.10 is standard issue and stays free: the six original looks and the seven original finishes.
- Purchases are cosmetic only. Nothing bought changes how a gun shoots.
- Credits and purchases are kept in `ferrite-client.txt` on your machine, with your player id. Like the id, they are taken on trust: servers pay credits out but do not check what a client wears.

## Getting started

A first launch opens on a four-step introduction: choose a callsign and icon, see the four modes, check the controls (and switch touch controls on), and learn how credits and streaks work. It ends with a welcome bonus of 400 credits and an offer of a practice match: a six-a-side deathmatch against Recruit bots. Every step can be skipped, and it is shown once. Players updating from an earlier version see it once too, and get the bonus.

## Foliage and atmosphere

Every classic map used to share one sky, one sun and the same thin grass. Each now has its own hour:

| Map | Hour | In the air |
|---|---|---|
| Foundry Valley | Gold afternoon, late in the year | Falling leaves, pollen, a little mist in the hollows |
| Quarry | White noon | Dust blowing along the ground, haze |
| Ridgeline | Cold early morning | Mist lying under the pines |
| Depot | Sunset behind the stacks | Lit dust, long shadows |
| Spillway | Overcast, no sun to speak of | Mist over the drained channel |

- **Undergrowth.** Bushes (half of them gathered at the foot of trees and rocks), drifts of tall grass, flower patches, ferns under trees, dry scrub, reeds and cattails in the lowest ground, stumps and windfall, saplings, fallen leaves under every tree, ivy up concrete walls, moss on boulders and weeds along the foot of every wall. Each map takes its own mix. Relay gains heather and highland grass; Slag Cathedral is unchanged.
- **The forest beyond the fence** is several times thicker and taller, so a map ends in trees and not a bare slope, and far ranges stand on the horizon behind it.
- **Wind.** Grass, flowers, reeds and bush tops sway, in gusts that cross the field.
- **Skies.** Each sky has its own colours, a glow on the sun's side, clouds lit to match and, at the Spillway, an overcast.
- **Light.** The colour of the sun and of the shade is set by the map: warm and low at the Depot, weak and even at the Spillway.
- Nothing planted collides or stops a bullet, and the server knows nothing about it. Anything big enough to hide behind (bushes, tall grass, reeds, saplings) is planted at a spot and at its mirror image, so both teams get the same cover. Bots do not see foliage: they will spot you in grass that hides them from you. Objectives and spawn ground are kept clear.
- The static world is about 700,000 triangles on the leafiest maps, up from about 420,000.

## Touch controls

On-screen controls for touch screens: a thumb stick on the left that appears where your thumb lands (pushed right out, it sprints), drag anywhere on the right to look, and buttons for fire, aim, jump, crouch, reload, swap, frag, melee and sight, with pause, scoreboard and loadout top right. Holding FIRE and dragging aims while you shoot. AIM and CROUCH are tap-on, tap-off. Each finger is tracked separately, so moving, aiming and firing work together.

They switch themselves on at the first touch of a touch screen. To try them on a desktop, start with `--touch` or turn on SETTINGS > CONTROLS > MOUSE > TOUCH CONTROLS: the left mouse button stands in for one finger. A keyboard and a controller keep working alongside.

This release does not include a phone or tablet build. Still to do for one: an Android / iOS package, an on-screen keyboard for the callsign and chat, and a HUD scale for small screens (the layout fits a tablet; a phone needs smaller panels).

## Compatibility

The network protocol is now 16. Everyone in a match needs v0.10.0, including the dedicated server.
